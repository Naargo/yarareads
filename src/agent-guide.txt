YARA READS: A GUIDE FOR AGENTS ADDING BOOKS

Yara Reads is a reading app for children of about 5 to 7 who have just learned their phonics sounds. A child taps any
word and its sound buttons appear. You are adding books to one parent's private account. Only that account sees them.
This guide is what we learned making the app's own illustrated series (Julia and Toby, 7 chapters). Follow it.

THE ONE RULE
Every word in a story must be readable by the child: either it can be sounded out with the sounds the account has
switched on, or it is one of the account's "sight words" (read on sight), or it is a character name you list in `names`.
The app checks by pronunciation, not just spelling ("cake" fails if the child doesn't know the split vowel a_e).
Never ask the parent to loosen their sounds to make a story pass. Rewrite the story.

WORKFLOW
1. Ask the parent what the child loves (animals, dragons, a best friend, a place they know) and how long it should be.
2. get_sounds: see which sounds, sight words and names this account has. Write only with those.
3. Draft the story as short pages (see WRITING). Then check_text on all pages at once. It lists every unreadable word by page.
   Rewrite those words (a simpler word, or a different sentence) and check again. Repeat until nothing is unreadable.
4. save_book with the pages. For kind "story" it refuses text with unreadable words and tells you which.
   Saving again with the same title (or the id you were given) replaces the text and keeps existing pictures on pages that still exist.
5. Pictures (see PICTURES): set_image for the cover (page 0) and each page, from a URL or base64.
6. preview_url, then look at it (see CHECKING). Fix what you see and preview again.

WRITING
- The point: most books for new readers are repetitive and dull, so children stop reading them. Write something the child
  would choose: a character with a clear want, a problem, a small win. For a series, end each chapter on a hook.
- A series of short chapters beats one long book: 8 to 13 pages a chapter. Give the chapters the same `series` and
  numbers 1, 2, 3... Keep a short plan of the whole series (characters, places, what happens) and stay true to it.
- Pages are short: one to three sentences, under about 25 words. The child reads each page aloud on their own.
  A page with too many words makes the text shrink on a tablet: split it.
- Pick one tense and keep it everywhere, in every chapter. Present tense works best: "-ed" endings are usually not on a
  beginner's sound list. Watch for slips ("she runs... she ran") when you rewrite a sentence to pass check_text.
- Before drafting, make a word bank: send your likely words (names of things in the story, verbs, feelings) to check_text
  as one page and keep what passes. Many easy-looking words fail: "happy" and "mountain" use sounds a beginner may not
  have; "scared", "jumped" need -ed. Say it another way ("peak" for mountain, "glad" for happy, "fear" for scared).
- `names` are for characters and named places only (Julia, Toby, Super-Tall Peak). Never list an ordinary word as a name to
  get it through: the child would meet a word she can't read.
- Feelings carry the story; name them in words she can read (glad, sad, mad, fear, joy).
- Safe for any five-year-old: gentle peril, quick comfort, no cruelty. Villains are grumpy and mysterious, not monstrous.
- Give each page a `picture`: a short visual description of the scene for the illustrator (who, doing what, where, mood).
  It's never shown to the child, and it is the main lever for a good picture.

PICTURES
Shape and size
- Landscape 4:3 (for example 1536x1152 or 1024x768), at least 1024 pixels wide. Send the full-quality image you have,
  JPEG, PNG or WebP up to 3 MB: the server makes smaller copies sized for each screen, so don't shrink it yourself.
- The app fills the picture box and crops: up to about a fifth off any side, a third on a page with lots of text.
  Keep faces and the action in the middle; nothing important at the edges.
- No words, letters or signs in the picture: the child will try to read them.
Keeping characters the same on every page
- Write one style line for the whole series (ours: "Children's picture book illustration. Soft watercolor with gentle
  colored-pencil lines, warm light, rounded friendly shapes, simple uncluttered backgrounds, expressive faces.
  No text anywhere in the image.") and a fixed description of each character, object and place. Reuse them word for word.
- If your image tool takes reference images, first draw a character sheet for each recurring character (full body, plain
  background), pick the best, and pass it with every picture they appear in.
The method that worked best for us: a board per chapter as the plan, then each page drawn on its own
- Ask for the whole chapter as ONE image: a 4x4 grid of 16 panels separated by thin white gutters, given the chapter's full
  text, the style line and the character sheets. Panels drawn together keep their continuity (light, props, the same garden).
  Draw it as large as your tool allows (we use 3072x2304). Pass the previous chapter's board as a reference for the next.
- Choose the panel whose moment fits each page best. Spare panels are the point: they give you choices.
- Don't use the panels as the pictures. They're small, and small figures go wrong: a character drawn twice, a wing that
  looks like a leg, a leg vanishing into a gate. Instead, redraw each page on its own at full size with its panel as a
  reference: "Image 1 is a small sketch of this page. Draw it again as a finished full-page illustration: the same place,
  the same moment, the same characters in the same spots. Draw each character once, with natural, correct anatomy",
  plus the character sheets and the page's words. Draw two takes and keep the better one.
- Keep instructions short and let the model choose the pose. When a take fails, ask for the simplest pose that tells the
  moment (running past the gate, not mid-jump over it). Long, detailed pose instructions made things worse for us.
- If your tool can't do boards, draw page by page with the same style line, descriptions and sheets every time.
Check every picture before set_image
- Look at each one at full size, zoomed in on the characters. Reject it if:
  - it isn't fine for any five-year-old: no fangs, snarls, gore or realistic horror; the style stays soft (a picture that
    slides into dark-fantasy realism is wrong even if the content is mild);
  - the anatomy is wrong: count arms, legs, hands, feet and fingers; knees and elbows bend the right way;
  - a character appears twice, or a body part reads as another, or a limb disappears into an object;
  - it doesn't show what the page says at a glance;
  - a character is off-model (different colours, clothes, size) or a place doesn't match earlier pages.
- A failure gets another take, or a clearer, shorter description of a simpler pose.
The cover
- Use one of the book's own pictures. Choose one that is distinct for this chapter but doesn't give the plot away:
  not the twist, not the villain's big moment.

CHECKING
preview_url opens the app as this account for an hour. If you have a browser tool, page through the whole book at tablet
sizes, landscape 1180x820 and portrait 820x1180, and on a phone (390x844). Check: the text is large (if it has shrunk,
the page has too many words), no face or key action is cropped off, every picture fits its page, and tapping a word
shows its sound buttons. Then tell the parent what you made and ask them to read it with the child.

TWO KINDS OF BOOK
- kind "story" (default): written for this child, so every word must be readable. save_book enforces it.
- kind "book": a real published book the family owns, typed in as pages. Its words are not checked; the app marks the words the
  child can't sound out yet with a dotted underline, and the parent sees what share is readable. Only add books the parent says they own.
  The book stays private to their account. Use the book's own pictures (photos or scans of its pages, cropped to the illustration).

LIMITS
At most 40 books per account, 60 pages per book, 600 characters per page. Images up to 3 MB.
